# Measured colour model of the panel

A separate, earlier strand of the project: a forward model that predicts how an sRGB image will look on the Gallery 3 panel when rendered by the stock software. It came from photographing the panel, not from the waveform work, and it treats the panel as a black box.

Code and data are in [`panel-model/`](../panel-model/).

## How it was built

1. **Photograph** the panel showing a known colour image (phone camera, indoor light).
2. **Rectify** the photo onto the source image: SIFT features, ratio-test matching and a RANSAC homography (`rectify.py`), giving a pixel-aligned 810 × 1080 photo.
3. **White-balance** the photo on pixels that are near-neutral and bright in the source.
4. **Measure** (`measure.py` → `measurements.json`): binned tone response (source lightness → photographed lightness), chroma retention and hue shift per hue band, and the highest chroma the panel reached per hue.
5. **Fit** (`fit_model.py` → `model.json`), in CIE Lab:
   - Tone: a monotone binned map of L, plus a linear fit for reference.
   - Chroma: per hue band (24 bands), a retention ratio at moderate chroma and a ceiling (98th percentile), with a soft saturation `C_out = ceiling × tanh(ratio × C / ceiling)`.
   - Hue: a per-band shift, damped where the panel reached little chroma.
6. **Simulate** (`panel_sim.py`): apply the model to any image, plus a slight blur to stand in for the dither.

## What the model says

The fitted tone map (in `model.json`) compresses the panel's range heavily: source lightness 0-100 maps to roughly 29-83 in the white-balanced photo. Chroma retention and ceilings vary strongly by hue. Read the numbers in `model.json` rather than a summary here.

## Limits

- **One photo, one light.** The model was fitted from a single photo under one indoor light. A second photo taken in a different room gave chroma ceilings that were not comparable, so that refit is not included.
- It describes the panel **as photographed and white-balanced**, not as measured with a colorimeter.
- It models the stock renderer's output (dither included), not individual ink states.
- The source image used for the fit is third-party art and is not included, so `measurements.json` and `model.json` cannot be regenerated byte for byte from this repository. The scripts take any source image and matching photo as arguments.
